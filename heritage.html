Ethiopia’s history is rich in science and astronomy. This page highlights documented traditions and observations that tie Ethiopia’s heritage to the stars.

CULTURAL ASTRONOMY

Stars in Ethiopia’s Past

Ancient Ethiopian cultures integrated astronomy into daily life. For example, **Axumite** stelae and tombs were often aligned with the solstices and equinoxes, showing intentional celestial design. The **Borena** people traditionally use a sophisticated lunar-stellar calendar for agriculture, tied to the risings of bright stars. Even Ethiopia’s famed rock-hewn churches (e.g. Lalibela) show alignments to sunrise on major feast days.

  • Axumite Alignments: Ancient monuments aligned with solstice/equinox positions.
  • Borena Calendar: A precise lunar-stellar farming calendar, confirmed by recent studies.
  • Lalibela Churches: Solar alignments on Christian festival dates.
  • Manuscript Astronomy: Many Geʽez manuscripts (computus books) include astronomical tables and diagrams.

These examples are documented by archaeology and anthropological astronomy. Below we separate **verified modern links** from **investigational claims** about Ethiopia’s sky heritage.

NAMES IN THE SKY

Verified Ethiopian Names

Ethiopia’s presence in modern astronomy appears in official naming campaigns and space projects. Notably, Ethiopia participated in the IAU NameExoWorlds contest (2019) and chose the names Buna and Abol for a star and its planet. These names (meaning “coffee” and “first brew”) are now internationally recognized. Ethiopia has also launched satellites into orbit and has an asteroid named after it.

ቡና BUNA

VERIFIED · STAR

Buna

HD 16175

The star HD 16175 was officially named Buna (“coffee” in Amharic) in 2019 during the IAU NameExoWorlds campaign. This gives Ethiopia a direct cultural link to this distant star.

✓ OFFICIALLY NAMED

Source: IAU NameExoWorlds (Ethiopia 2019)

አቦል ABOL

VERIFIED · EXOPLANET

Abol

HD 16175 b

The exoplanet HD 16175 b was named Abol (አቦል, “first brew”) alongside its star Buna in 2019. This honors Ethiopia’s traditional coffee ceremony within modern astronomy.

✓ OFFICIALLY NAMED

Source: IAU NameExoWorlds (Ethiopia 2019)

ኢትዮጵያ ETHIOPIA

VERIFIED · ASTEROID

(1432) Ethiopia

1937 PG

Asteroid (1432) Ethiopia is a main-belt asteroid discovered on August 1, 1937 by Cyril V. Jackson. Its name was chosen for Ethiopia (then Abyssinia) in honor of the country. This is a well-documented astronomical fact.

✓ DOCUMENTED RECORD

Source: Minor Planet Center; discovery by C. Jackson.

MODERN SPACE PROGRAM

From Observatory to Orbit

In recent years, Ethiopia has made strides in space science. The Ethiopian Space Science Society was founded in 2004, and the Entoto Observatory opened in 2013. Most notably, Ethiopia launched its first satellite, ETRSS-1, on December 20, 2019. This Earth-observation satellite was developed in cooperation with China and operates in low Earth orbit.

In 2022, Ethiopia established the Space Science and Geospatial Institute (SSGI) to coordinate space and geospatial efforts. The country continues to train engineers and expand its participation in international space projects.
